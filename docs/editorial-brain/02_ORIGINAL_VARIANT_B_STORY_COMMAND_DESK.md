# Original Variant B: Story Command Desk

## Thesis

The Original Live Desk becomes an editorial command desk organised around the strongest live market questions. Releases, evidence rooms, calendars, charts and history support those questions.

This variant is best when the primary problem is not missing information, but failing to turn information into a timely and specific article decision.

The first screen must answer:

> What changed, what matters now, what happens next and what should Lee do with it?

## Core experience

The homepage is a newsroom-style decision surface with one centrepiece story and a ranked queue.

The system still stores every release and component in full. It simply does not force the analyst to begin with a database view.

## Homepage structure

### 1. Since Last Visit

A strict delta feed shows only material changes since the user's last session.

Each item includes:

- exact time;
- specific development;
- source status;
- story changed;
- why the development is material;
- assets already reacting;
- whether the editorial decision changed.

Repeated background is excluded.

### 2. What Matters Now

One centrepiece Decision Brief contains:

- precise event;
- market reaction;
- what the market appears to believe;
- why that belief may be reasonable;
- overlooked variable;
- evidence for and against;
- market pricing versus physical or fundamental confirmation;
- next catalyst and expected time;
- relevant assets;
- best chart;
- article recommendation.

The title is event-first and time-aware.

Weak:

> Oil is pricing diplomacy before physical normalisation.

Stronger:

> Rubio pushed back on an imminent Iran agreement while Brent continued to price one.

The persistent thesis remains underneath the event headline.

### 3. Today, Tonight and Friday

The calendar is rewritten as an impact map.

For each event:

- local time;
- expected result or decision;
- prior and revision risk;
- current market pricing;
- active stories affected;
- surprise thresholds;
- direct assets;
- what outcome would genuinely change the view.

Examples include NFP, CPI, ISM, central-bank decisions, earnings, policy deadlines and diplomatic milestones.

### 4. Editorial Queue

Every promoted story receives one action:

| Action | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lead now | Strongest current OB or breaking angle |
| Develop today | Worth additional research before publication |
| Prepare conditional | Draft scenarios around an upcoming catalyst |
| COTD | Chart-led focused opportunity |
| Add to OB | Supporting section rather than main story |
| Wait | Important but evidence incomplete |
| Monitor | Background story with no current trigger |
| Cool | Materiality is fading |
| Resolve | Thesis settled or catalyst passed |

The queue must state why the action changed.

### 5. Competing Angles

For the centrepiece story, the system proposes two or three credible editorial angles.

Each angle includes:

- central question;
- supporting evidence;
- main weakness;
- required chart;
- overlap with recent Alchemy coverage;
- likely format.

This gives Lee options without making him repeat the discovery process in a separate chat.

### 6. Supporting intelligence

Below the editorial surface sit:

- release lab;
- evidence room;
- statement comparison;
- physical confirmation;
- chart room;
- historical analogues;
- article memory;
- story journey;
- research ledger.

These modules support the story rather than competing for homepage attention.

## Story object

A story is not only a theme. It stores both a persistent thesis and a live event layer.

### Persistent layer

- stable slug;
- central market question;
- base thesis;
- causal mechanism;
- affected assets;
- structural evidence;
- long-term confirmation and invalidation.

### Live layer

- latest event headline;
- what changed;
- exact update time;
- claim status;
- current market reaction;
- evidence delta;
- next catalyst;
- editorial action;
- freshness status.

This prevents broad hypotheses from appearing as if they are today's news.

## Editorial-brain sequence

### Step 1: Detect the event

The system detects a new release, statement, market move, physical observation or scheduled catalyst.

### Step 2: Compare against the active story

It asks:

- Is the information genuinely new?
- Does it change the balance of evidence?
- Does it resolve an unknown?
- Is it contradicted by another party?
- Is the market reacting before confirmation?

### Step 3: Run the market-is-right pass

The system states the strongest rational explanation for current pricing.

### Step 4: Find the gap

It looks for:

- missing implementation;
- ignored revision;
- weak subcomponent;
- physical contradiction;
- positioning mismatch;
- policy-path mismatch;
- timing problem;
- an upcoming event that has not been integrated.

### Step 5: Build the mechanism

The story must show how the event reaches growth, inflation, rates, earnings, liquidity or physical supply, and then how it reaches the named assets.

### Step 6: Rank editorial value

The system scores:

- market impact;
- immediacy;
- expectation gap;
- evidence quality;
- contradiction quality;
- testability;
- chartability;
- originality;
- article usefulness.

The visible output is a decision label, not a false-precision score.

## Data release integration

Every major release still receives the full Release Ledger treatment.

The Story Command Desk surfaces only the release elements relevant to the centrepiece question.

Example:

If NFP is the main story, the centrepiece might foreground:

- headline payrolls;
- revisions;
- wages;
- hours;
- unemployment;
- claims and JOLTS cross-checks;
- Fed-path impact;
- DXY, Treasury and USDJPY reaction.

The complete table remains one click below.

If an Iran-Oman negotiation is the main story, the desk foregrounds:

- exact agreement stage;
- parties confirming or contradicting it;
- timeline;
- IRGC conditions;
- vessel and insurance evidence;
- oil curve and product-market reaction;
- next official checkpoint.

The difference from Variant A is placement. The page is a specialist workbench rather than the default homepage.

## History Cabinet

The cabinet is organised by both story and release.

A user can enter through:

- a story timeline;
- a release family;
- an asset;
- an article;
- a catalyst;
- a methodology review.

The cabinet preserves what the desk believed at each point, not only the final corrected view.

## Maintenance model

### Shared templates

Decision Briefs use one schema across geopolitical, macro, company and market-move stories.

### Automated deltas

The system automatically identifies changed fields and drafts a factual delta summary.

### Analyst exceptions

The analyst confirms:

- materiality;
- the decisive contradiction;
- the best article angle;
- the relevant assets;
- whether the next catalyst is truly decisive.

### Story lifecycle

Stories move through:

- Candidate;
- Researching;
- Priority;
- Developing;
- Waiting;
- Background;
- Cooling;
- Resolved;
- Archived.

A story cannot stay Priority merely because it remains important in general.

## Strengths

- Best answer to the current usefulness problem.
- Places article decisions ahead of system metrics.
- Works for macro, earnings and geopolitics.
- Integrates calendars into active narratives.
- Makes the desk feel timely.
- Preserves all detailed research underneath.

## Weaknesses

- Requires strong ranking and freshness logic.
- Can hide valuable release detail if navigation is weak.
- A poor story-selection model can distort the whole homepage.
- Requires careful handling of event-driven updates.

## Best use

Use this variant as the Original's **default homepage and editorial operating layer**. Pair it with Variant A's Release Ledger and History Cabinet.
