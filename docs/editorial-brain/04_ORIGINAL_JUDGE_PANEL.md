# Original Live Desk Judge Panel

## Purpose

This panel judges the three Original Live Desk variants independently. The goal is not to select one complete design. The goal is to identify which modules best solve the research, editorial and maintenance problems.

Variants:

- **A: Release Ledger First**
- **B: Story Command Desk**
- **C: Macro Operating System**

## Judge 1: Senior Macro Strategist

### Priorities

- Correct interpretation of economic releases.
- Cross-release consistency.
- Policy reaction functions.
- Distinction between leading, coincident and lagging evidence.
- Ability to explain why a release changes or does not change the macro view.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 9.2 | Excellent release detail and revision history. Needs a stronger cross-release state layer. |
| B | 8.3 | Strong editorial focus, but risks over-weighting the latest event. |
| C | 9.5 | Best macro synthesis and best protection against reacting to one noisy release. |

### Selection

Use Variant C for macro state and Variant A for release decomposition.

## Judge 2: Opening Bell Editor

### Priorities

- Finds today's strongest story quickly.
- Produces specific, publishable angles.
- Integrates calendar events with live stories.
- Distinguishes background from new developments.
- Reduces the need to open a separate research discussion.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 7.8 | Very useful for data-led days, but the story can be buried inside the release. |
| B | 9.7 | Directly answers what changed, what matters and what to write. |
| C | 8.5 | Strong context, but the state-machine language can slow the editorial decision. |

### Selection

Use Variant B as the homepage and editorial queue.

## Judge 3: Data Provenance Auditor

### Priorities

- Raw and interpreted records remain separate.
- Revisions are preserved.
- Every conclusion can be reconstructed.
- Claim status and source tier are visible.
- Historical records are not overwritten.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 9.8 | Strongest auditability and vintage handling. |
| B | 8.8 | Good if every Decision Brief links back to the canonical evidence. |
| C | 9.1 | Strong state history, but evidence-weight calculations require transparent governance. |

### Selection

Use Variant A's History Cabinet and append-only release objects as the record foundation.

## Judge 4: Research Operations Engineer

### Priorities

- Easy maintenance.
- Low duplication.
- Reusable schemas and templates.
- Scheduled and event-driven updates.
- Clear failure states and source-health handling.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 9.4 | Release templates are highly maintainable and automate well. |
| B | 8.7 | Decision Briefs are reusable, but editorial ranking requires more judgement and monitoring. |
| C | 8.4 | Powerful reuse, but state weights and mappings create additional maintenance. |

### Selection

Use Variant A's templated ingestion, Variant B's shared Decision Brief schema and a deliberately small Variant C state dictionary.

## Judge 5: Lee as Power User

### Priorities

- Saves research time.
- Surfaces timely and interesting stories.
- Provides enough detail to trust the recommendation.
- Makes release components understandable.
- Retains a persistent record for later writing.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 9.0 | Strongest for recurring ISM, CPI, NFP and release work. |
| B | 9.8 | Closest to the desired editorial brain and daily writing workflow. |
| C | 8.9 | Valuable for understanding interaction, but should stay behind the story layer. |

### Selection

Use Variant B first, Variant A one click below and Variant C as the logic connecting releases.

## Judge 6: Alchemy Client-Facing Standards Editor

### Priorities

- Avoids overstatement.
- Separates fact, pricing, inference and scenario.
- Supports British English and mobile-friendly copy.
- Produces confirmation and invalidation rather than slogans.
- Does not turn a data print into an unsupported trade call.

### Scores

| Variant | Score / 10 | Judgement |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 9.1 | Component detail supports careful writing. Requires concise output views. |
| B | 9.3 | Best narrative structure if the evidence gate remains binding. |
| C | 9.0 | Strong conditional language, but regime labels must not sound more certain than the evidence. |

### Selection

Use Variant B's narrative structure with Variant A's evidence details and Variant C's conditional state language.

## Weighted result

Weights reflect the desk's purpose of reducing research time while increasing credibility.

| Criterion | Weight | A | B | C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Editorial usefulness | 25% | 7.8 | 9.7 | 8.5 |
| Research depth | 20% | 9.5 | 8.8 | 9.5 |
| Timeliness | 15% | 8.3 | 9.6 | 8.4 |
| Auditability | 15% | 9.8 | 8.8 | 9.1 |
| Maintainability | 15% | 9.4 | 8.7 | 8.4 |
| Historical value | 10% | 9.8 | 8.7 | 9.4 |
| **Weighted score** | **100%** | **9.0** | **9.1** | **8.9** |

The scores are close because the variants solve different layers.

## Panel verdict

Do not choose one full variant.

Build the Original as:

1. **Variant B homepage and editorial queue.**
2. **Variant A release workbench and History Cabinet.**
3. **Variant C macro-state engine and cross-release interaction layer.**

This combination gives the desk a strong editorial centre without sacrificing detail, persistence or methodological depth.

## Rejected combinations

### Variant A alone

Too likely to become a comprehensive but passive macro database.

### Variant B alone

Too dependent on story ranking and vulnerable to weak release decomposition.

### Variant C alone

Too abstract for daily Opening Bell production.

### Separate systems for each variant

Rejected because it would duplicate data, create inconsistent interpretations and increase maintenance.

## Conditions for approval

The panel approves the combined design only if:

- the same canonical release and story objects power all three layers;
- the full research methods remain binding;
- the homepage shows event-specific deltas rather than broad theme summaries;
- all release views preserve vintages and revisions;
- asset impacts pass a relevance gate;
- the History Cabinet records the interpretation that existed at the time;
- source-health and staleness are visible;
- event-driven updates supplement the four scheduled cycles.
