# Original Live Desk: Recommended Editorial-Brain Blueprint

## Decision

The Original should not be rebuilt as one monolithic dashboard. It should combine the strongest modules from all three variants:

- **Homepage and daily decisions:** Story Command Desk.
- **Economic-release analysis:** Release Ledger.
- **Cross-release synthesis:** Macro Operating System.
- **Persistent memory:** shared History Cabinet.

The Original remains the most detailed product. It owns the complete research record, including raw sources, claims, component observations, causal links, story versions, charts, editorial decisions and post-event reviews.

## Product hierarchy

### Level 1: Editorial command surface

This is what Lee sees first.

It answers:

1. What changed since the last visit?
2. What matters most now?
3. What should be watched today, tonight or later in the week?
4. What does the market appear to be pricing?
5. What is confirmed, contradicted or still missing?
6. What deserves an OB, COTD, monitor or no action?

### Level 2: Research workbenches

Specialist pages provide the full detail:

- Latest Data Releases;
- Release Lab;
- ISM component room;
- Inflation and Fed reaction function;
- Evidence Room;
- Statement comparison;
- Physical confirmation;
- Earnings expectations room;
- Chart room;
- Catalyst calendar;
- Article memory.

### Level 3: Persistent macro and story state

This layer connects evidence across time:

- growth;
- inflation;
- labour;
- policy;
- liquidity and credit;
- oil and physical supply;
- risk appetite;
- USD;
- yen and carry;
- AI capex.

### Level 4: History Cabinet and audit trail

Every release, story, state and article decision is retrievable by vintage.

## Recommended homepage

### A. Since Last Visit

Only material deltas.

Required fields:

- time;
- event;
- exact new fact;
- verification status;
- story affected;
- why it matters;
- market reaction;
- editorial action change.

### B. What Matters Now

One centrepiece story.

Required blocks:

- event headline;
- current thesis;
- what changed;
- expectation map;
- why the market may be right;
- overlooked variable;
- evidence for;
- evidence against;
- mechanism;
- market pricing versus reality;
- next catalyst;
- confirmation;
- invalidation;
- relevant assets;
- best chart;
- editorial recommendation.

### C. Today, Tonight and Later This Week

Calendar events are grouped by when they can alter an active story.

Each event includes:

- local time;
- consensus or expected decision;
- prior and revision risk;
- active stories affected;
- surprise thresholds;
- most relevant assets;
- what outcome would change the view.

### D. Editorial Queue

Three to seven items maximum:

- Lead now;
- Develop today;
- Prepare conditional;
- COTD;
- Add to OB;
- Wait;
- Monitor.

Cooling and resolved stories sit below the active queue.

### E. Latest Data Release

A compact release card links to the full workbench.

It shows:

- actual, consensus, prior and revision;
- decisive component;
- Inflation check;
- Fed check;
- relevant asset;
- state change;
- story update.

### F. Macro State Delta

A compact strip shows only states that changed.

### G. Supporting modules

Market Pulse, Story Map, Top Signals, Trend Preview, Article Memory and system health remain, but sit beneath the decision surface.

## ISM component room

The ISM room includes:

1. Headline PMI.
2. New Orders.
3. Production.
4. Employment.
5. Supplier Deliveries.
6. Inventories.
7. Prices.
8. Backlog of Orders.
9. New Export Orders.
10. Imports.

Optional services-specific components can be configured separately.

### Required interpretation sequence

1. Identify the main driver of the headline move.
2. Determine whether demand and output agree.
3. Determine whether Employment confirms or lags.
4. Explain whether slower Supplier Deliveries reflect demand or disruption.
5. Explain whether Prices indicate broadening pipeline pressure.
6. Determine whether inventory behaviour is planned or unwanted.
7. Compare with regional surveys, payrolls, claims, CPI, PPI and market pricing where relevant.
8. State whether the release changes the Fed path.
9. Name only the directly relevant assets.
10. Store the vintage in the History Cabinet.

## Relevance gate for asset impacts

An asset is displayed only when all of the following are true:

1. The causal link is direct enough to explain in one sentence.
2. The horizon is specified.
3. The asset is currently sensitive to the relevant driver.
4. The move or pricing can be checked.
5. The asset adds information rather than filling a template.

The system should prefer three strong asset links over ten generic arrows.

## Processing and maintenance

### One write path

All writes occur in the Original.

### Derived views

Original pages and Hybrid pages use generated presentation snapshots.

### Release templates

Every release family has a stable component dictionary and cross-check map.

### Story templates

Every story uses the same Decision Brief schema, with optional specialist modules.

### State mappings

A small governed dictionary maps evidence to macro states.

### Four scheduled cycles

- 00:40 video and overnight intake;
- 08:30 full desk;
- 11:30 refresh;
- 22:00 evening delta.

### Event-driven updates

Triggered by:

- high-impact release;
- policy decision;
- earnings event;
- verified material statement;
- physical-flow change;
- large cross-asset move;
- contradiction to a priority story.

### Failure handling

The interface displays:

- stale source;
- missing release component;
- unresolved contradiction;
- failed market-data fetch;
- delayed transcript;
- cabinet write failure;
- Hybrid snapshot lag.

It must never silently reuse an old conclusion as current.

## Acceptance tests

### ISM test

The page must explain whether a headline move came from demand, production, supply delays, prices or inventories, and whether that changes inflation, Fed pricing or only the manufacturing view.

### NFP test

The page must integrate headline payrolls, revisions, unemployment, wages and hours with claims, JOLTS, Treasury pricing and active Fed or yen stories.

### Oman or Iran deal test

The desk must identify the reported framework, parties, stage, contradictions, implementation requirements, physical confirmation, market pricing, timeline and next checkpoint.

### Maintenance test

A revised value updates the Original, Hybrid and History Cabinet without manual duplication.

### Usefulness test

Within sixty seconds, Lee can identify the lead story, the decisive evidence, the next catalyst and the recommended output.

## Final recommendation

The Original should feel like a complete institutional research room with an editor at the front desk.

Depth remains available everywhere. The difference is that the system now processes that depth into a timely editorial decision before asking the user to navigate the supporting equipment.
