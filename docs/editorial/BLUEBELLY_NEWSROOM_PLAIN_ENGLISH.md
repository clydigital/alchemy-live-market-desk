# Bluebelly Newsroom — plain-English article standard
**Effective 24 November 2026. Applies to all newly written or revised public Newsroom explainers, rolling-topic articles, source-qualified event readers and related Story articles.**

## Reader promise
The answer must be clear in about a minute, without removing the underlying research. Readers should understand **what happened, why it matters to markets or households, and what to watch next** before they encounter source audits, econometric terms, model state or long chronology.

### Default visible structure
1. **Plain headline + one-sentence takeaway.** Say what changed and whom it affects. Avoid taxonomy/status codes in the headline.
2. **What happened?** Two to four short sentences. Give dated, source-verified facts. Show only the one to three most decision-relevant comparable numbers; if the event hinges on consensus, show *actual versus expected* with date and units.
3. **Why does it matter?** Two to four short sentences. Explain the economic route in human terms — who pays, earns, borrows or bears a risk — then name the affected assets only when grounded.
4. **What should I watch next?** One or two observable events or conditions that would strengthen or weaken the explanation. Do not invent a trigger.

Treat these as **three content questions**, not mandatory repeated numbered or badge-heavy chapter headings. The initial answer should not contain long legalistic evidence caveats, method taxonomy, multiple duplicate cards, audit IDs or a glossary wall.

### Optional depth, collapsed by default
Keep original evidence, exact publication/observation timestamps, expectation vintages, all useful source links, issuer comparisons, mechanism diagrams, counterarguments and verification warnings in a clearly labelled **Read more: data, sources and alternative explanations** area. An original source link should also remain easy to reach without opening the advanced view. Detailed data is still required; it is progressively disclosed, never deleted or replaced by a claim without backing.

### Writing requirements
- Target a sensible general reader; use everyday words. Explain necessary terms **on first mention**: e.g., *credit spread (extra interest lenders demand)*, *free cash flow (cash left after essential investment)*, *PCE (a US inflation measure watched by the Fed)*.
- Use direct verbs, short paragraphs, no more than one substantive point per paragraph. Use British English. Prioritise clarity over insider shorthand.
- Each figure needs a unit, comparable period and source. When a forecast is relevant, compare the measured figure to the **pre-release forecast**, not merely the previous observation.
- Separate **confirmed fact**, **plausible explanation**, **verified price reaction** and **unknown**. A source announcing a policy decision does not by itself prove a market price reaction or its cause.
- Give the strongest alternative explanation when material, without false certainty or automatic thesis changes.
- If the key effect remains unverified, say simply: *We know the announcement happened; we cannot yet confirm how much of the price move it caused.*
- Avoid printing raw machine status labels such as UNRESOLVED or SOURCE_CORROBORATED where ordinary language works.
- Use charts or mini-tables only where they replace prose and help someone answer a question; no ornamental technical dashboards in the article lead.
- Do not imply current prices in a frozen edition. Cite dated sources and mark missing evidence.
- Never invent numbers, dates, consensus, sources or confirmed cause, and do not lower the existing evidence/publication gates.

## Manual news additions — context and ongoing research (24 November)
Every manually researched or added Newsroom item must answer, in natural prose rather than a rigid template:

- **Who is involved?** Introduce unfamiliar companies, products, institutions or people on first mention. Give their actual role (supplier, buyer, financier, regulator, competitor, etc.), what they sell or do, and why the reader should care. Do not assume a ticker or acronym explains the company.
- **Who is affected?** Where material, explain the customer, supplier, competitor or financing relationship and how money, demand, supply, costs or risk travel between them. Named relationships require sources; unnamed counterparties stay unnamed. Explain potential beneficiaries and exposures conditionally, not as automatic stock recommendations.
- **What changes in our market thinking?** Identify the relevant active Regime Lens or running Story/thesis and its specific question. Check the current research before writing; say whether the new evidence strengthens, weakens, complicates or leaves that question unresolved, and why. Link directly to the relevant existing lens and, when an exact current Story exists, its canonical reader. Do not create a thesis solely to attach a link or imply its verdict has changed.
- **What would settle it?** State the next observable test. Compare with existing Markets Compare/divergence evidence, dates, prices and relevant fundamentals where material. Do not infer a market-price cause from timing or a creator's opinion.

Keep the first read compact: usually one short explanation of the players and customer economics integrated into the existing story, followed by a brief research connection. No mandatory cards or headings for every question. Retain sources and detailed comparisons in optional depth. If no material running thesis applies, omit the link rather than force a connection. Manually written news and source checks remain dated and evidence-labelled; research leads cannot become verified facts or automatically change a canonical regime.

## Editorial quality check before publishing
Can someone outside financial markets identify the news, who the players are, their customers or affected parties, the stakes, the relevant research question, and the next test within a minute? Is at least one dated original report accessible? Are actual and expected figures comparable when used? Is an unverified market reaction clearly identified? Can a specialist still open the detailed sources and verify all meaningful claims? If any answer is no, revise the article layout rather than dropping evidence.

## Example
**Overly technical:** “Front-end repricing divergence and transmission have not been independently established by the evidence ledger.”

**Plain English:** “The Fed's announcement is confirmed. We still need to check whether two-year Treasury yields moved afterwards before saying the decision changed rate expectations.”
