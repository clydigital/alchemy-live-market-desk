**Companion:** `docs/editorial/BLUEBELLY_STORY_DRIVEN_DAILY_REPORTS.md` (v1.0), `docs/editorial/BLUEBELLY_CREATOR_STORY_SUMMARY_STANDARD.md` (v1.0), LY Style Guide and original research-source contracts.

## Editorial intent

Make a market article as absorbing as a Shark Tank pitch while keeping the author's real voice and analytical process: **the opportunity is introduced, the figures make the case, an objection changes the terms of the argument, and the reader follows the evidence to a conditional verdict.** The point is not theatrical drama or a finance textbook. It is to make the numbers *change what we think*.

**Do not replace LY's original phrasing or personal cadence by default.** Lightly smooth transitions, clarify mechanisms and preserve the author's paragraph flow, first-person judgements, technical levels, charts and **all existing hyperlinks**. Do not insert extra links into a user's supplied article unless specifically requested. Independent source receipts belong in the supporting research ledger/notes and may be added to new research-led drafts as required by the publication brief.

## 1. The pitch / money / catch / verdict engine

Treat each company or thesis like a deal being evaluated by an investor.

1. **The pitch — What is the opportunity?** Lead with one specific market dilemma or contradiction: stock down but earnings momentum improving, share price up while free cash flow stalls, bank holds rates while future hikes are priced in. State what the market appears to believe, versus what the reader is being asked to consider.
2. **The money — Show the economics.** Prove the pitch with *decision-relevant* figures, not a data dump. For company stories, prioritise revenue and growth, segment contribution and growth, operating profit/margin, operating cash flow, capex, free cash flow, balance sheet/financing costs, valuation *where verified and comparable*, and the current expectations already in the price. Do not pretend every story needs every metric.
3. **The catch — What would make a sceptical investor hesitate?** Confront the most credible countercase using quantitative evidence: strong segment growth but negative FCF; positive earnings but valuation already rich; strong AI demand but capex consuming cash; oversold oscillator but support break or resistance overhead. Do not invent counterarguments purely for balance.
4. **The negotiation — Which new evidence changes the price or judgement?** For upcoming earnings, compare consensus to a truly comparable baseline and specify the release/guidance needed to strengthen or weaken the interpretation. Use a short explanation when a figure's accounting basis changes the story.
5. **The verdict — What would *I* do or watch, conditionally?** Preserve the owner's explicitly supplied personal bias, including staying on the sidelines; do not manufacture first-person opinions for an automated report. End with **base case → test → invalidation / alternative**, with precise observable figures and dates.

**Important:** These are narrative moves, not mandatory visible section labels. Client-facing copy should usually read as a natural story, not as “Scene 1 / Scene 2” or a rigid analyst checklist.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy: statistics that earn their place

For each consequential metric, collect:

`company | metric | value | unit | reporting period | GAAP/non-GAAP basis | previous comparable period | expected value if available | source URL | report/publication date | calculation | caveat | narrative role`

Use a primary source (issuer earnings release, 10-Q/10-K, investor presentation, SEC filing, official policy release) for reported historical numbers. Obtain consensus separately and mark the provider and as-of time. Price/technical levels need the actual instrument (cash index, futures, CFD, share ticker), chart timeframe, source and observation time.

**Evidence order:**

- Start with the question the investor needs answered. Select only the metrics able to change the answer.
- **Baseline:** Compare YoY or QoQ with the exact window. A $20bn number without an appropriate denominator or baseline is not an insight.
- **Economics:** Report margin and profit alongside revenue when growth could be unprofitable. Identify profit concentration where a segment carries much of the group's earnings.
- **Cash conversion:** Separate operating cash flow, capital expenditures and free cash flow. Show the calculation **FCF = operating cash flow − cash capex** only when company definitions agree; reconcile any adjusted FCF definition and the treatment of leases, acquisitions or financing.
- **Investment burden:** Capex/operating cash flow or FCF/margin can be more informative than capex alone. Capital-intensive growth is not automatically a failure; explain the reinvestment and payback thesis.
- **Market expectations:** State what analysts/markets expect *before* the event, then distinguish a good business result from a positive earnings surprise and from a positive stock reaction.
- **Valuation:** Include P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF, market cap, forward estimates or yield comparisons only when independently verifiable, dates/basis comparable and relevant. Never improvise valuation multiples or use GAAP EPS against adjusted EPS to claim a growth/decline.
- **Debt/dilution/financing:** If material, examine share issuance, net debt, maturities, share count, SBC and interest coverage. Explain the economic cost simply when it affects the deal.

### A practical density rule

Use **1–2 decisive numbers in the lead**; typically **3–5 decision-changing company metrics** in a company scene or a compact table; leave comprehensive supporting figures in an evidence expansion/source ledger. This is an editorial default, not a quota. If a number does not change the diagnosis, omit it.

### Numerically honest comparisons

- Label **reported GAAP** versus **consensus adjusted** EPS explicitly; prefer matching bases and periods.
- Strip out or flag one-off gains, investment remeasurements and unusual tax items before claiming underlying profitability changed.
- Avoid confusing revenue, bookings, ARR, backlog and recognised cash collections.
- A negative quarter of FCF is not necessarily negative annual FCF; state the measurement window.
- Never infer revenue growth from a percentage without confirming its denominator and comparable period.
- Separate fact, arithmetic derived from facts, interpretation and scenario.

## 3. Storytelling structure (flexible)

**Opening (2–4 sentences):** Present the contradiction with one or two numbers and ask a question that gives the reader a reason to continue. Preserve an authored attention-grabbing line if it genuinely fits.

**Beat A — The setup:** What has happened to price or fundamentals, and why is now a decision point? Use the owner's chart or verified event, not a fabricated scene.

**Beat B — The first promising clue:** Show one or two financial or technical numbers that support the idea. Interject the mechanism *here*, not in an introductory glossary.

**Beat C — The catch:** Reveal the number, valuation, accounting quirk, momentum condition or policy risk that challenges the apparently easy conclusion. Explain why the original bullish/bearish evidence is incomplete.

**Beat D — A competing deal:** Contrast with a second business or alternative exposure where it helps readers choose. A strong company can be a poor entry; a falling stock can be a better setup with worse current cash flow. Compare on relevant comparable periods.

**Beat E — What changes the verdict:** Upcoming earnings, guidance, price/EMA bands, yield reactions, next cash-flow print. State **if/then**, one major counterargument, and the owner's genuine bias or a neutral analyst assessment.

Never make future catalysts appear to have already occurred. Use verified chronological order for historical events; use **decision order** when analysing upcoming releases and label the timeline honestly.

## 4. Learning moments: explain exactly when necessary

Insert **up to 2–3 short explanations** only where they unlock the story. In client-facing prose, usually weave them into sentences rather than interrupting the reader with repeated coloured boxes.

**Example — Why can strong earnings still send a stock lower?** Investors buy future expectations. If the price already anticipates excellent results, merely meeting those expectations may disappoint. The test is guidance and the share-price reaction, not EPS in isolation.

**Example — Why can cloud growth coexist with negative FCF?** Operating cash flow may increase while property, equipment and infrastructure spending rises faster. Check the cash outlay, any lease adjustments, and whether the invested capacity begins producing higher future returns.

**Example — Why does a thin volume-profile zone matter?** It indicates less historical trading in that price region, which may permit faster travel if support fails. It does not guarantee a move; use the current instrument, market structure and confirmation.

## 5. LY voice safeguards

- British English; short, mobile-friendly paragraphs and varied sentence rhythm.
- Keep natural first-person phrasing **only** where the presenter has actually stated the view. No AI-generated impersonation of conviction.
- Preserve distinct judgments: Google can look oversold while Microsoft looks overbought and Amazon still needs a support retest.
- Keep technical levels, band colours, chart order and timeframes intact unless corrected from evidence and approved.
- Do not default to formulas such as “The real story is ...”, “It matters because ...”, “Here is the catch ...” on every paragraph; make the logic carry the tension naturally.
- No faux Shark Tank sharks, negotiations, quotes, artificial conflict, melodramatic imagery or broad investment calls.
- No generic filler, false symmetry or stock-market jargon without explanatory value.
- If polishing an existing LY piece, make the **smallest local edit** that improves comprehension; offer a separate transformational rewrite rather than overwriting the owner's article.
- Preserve the original source hyperlinks in edits. No additional hyperlinks without direction for an existing author-supplied article.

## 6. Quantitative investor-test card (internal, reusable)

For every company or core thesis, prepare this compact internal object. Client copy should not necessarily display the form.

| Field | Question | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch | Why should anyone care now? | Price move + catalyst, dated |
| Growth | Is demand actually accelerating? | Revenue/segment growth, comparable period |
| Economics | Is that growth profitable? | Operating profit/margins and baseline |
| Cash | Is growth producing or consuming cash? | OCF, capex, FCF, correct basis |
| Price paid | Has the market priced this in? | Verified valuation, consensus, momentum |
| Catch | Strongest quantitative objection? | Contradictory metric or technical test |
| Verdict | What changes our assessment? | Guide, release/time, technical level, invalidation |

**Verdict modes:** opportunity conditional / too early / already priced in / pass or watch / insufficient evidence. These are interpretations, **not automatic trade recommendations**.

## 7. Applied example: October 2026 Big Tech article

Reference draft: `Bluebelly_Shark_Tank_Fundamentals_Oct_2026.html`; published author source: [Colossal News Week — Alchemy Markets](https://alchemymarkets.com/education/market-insights/opening-bell/tech-stocks-fomc-oct-2026/).

- **Alphabet:** The pitch is unusually strong Cloud growth; the catch is cash investment outpacing operating cash generation in the reported quarter. The conclusion is neither “growth fake” nor “free cash flow will stay negative”; ask whether capex productivity and later cash conversion improve. Cross-check Q2 2026 issuer filings before quoting precise metrics.
- **Microsoft:** The pitch is durable Azure demand with positive cash generation despite infrastructure spending; the catch is a share price that had already rallied into the event. Explain **good business versus good stock entry**.
- **Amazon:** The pitch is AWS earnings contribution and improving cloud growth; the catch is that heavy infrastructure spending can push consolidated trailing FCF negative. Explain why segment profitability and cash conversion answer different questions.
- **Technical overlay:** Nasdaq support near 28,867, Google 200D EMA resistance, Microsoft 20D EMA support, Amazon weekly 50 EMA band: keep original chart contexts, instrument/timeframe and observation timestamps; do not copy old values into a future live report without a fresh reading.

## 8. Workflow ownership (no parallel editorial canon)

1. **MacroPulse / creators / research adapters — discovery:** gather catalysts, original source URLs and timestamps. Creator takes are leads, not independently verified facts.
2. **Research Gap / evidence gate — verification:** resolve financial period/basis, consensus provenance, missing data and claim status; keep source receipts and unverified notes.
3. **Bluebelly narrative compiler — writing:** read existing `BLUEBELLY_STORY_DRIVEN_DAILY_REPORTS.md` and this Investor Test for **company/fundamentals-relevant** work. Creator summaries use their separate companion. Do not silently apply a fundamentals-heavy template to every macro bulletin.
4. **Blue Desk Writing Brain (weekday 09:00 MYT) — private editorial slate:** produce 3 ranked ideas; a selected OB can use this investor test; a COTD remains chart-led and uses fundamentals only when they alter the setup. Never invent presenter opinions.
5. **Bluebelly Stories / Daily / Presenter Prep — reading surfaces:** expose the short story first, with evidence and company metrics expandable. Present chart levels and next tests; keep private notes private. Publication still requires author review/approval.
6. **Alchemy Research Suite / Research Workflows plugin consumers:** route inputs toward Bluebelly, retain receipts and canonical Story/Dossier IDs, and refer to this Markdown by repository path rather than embedding duplicated instructions. Legacy Live/Hybrid stays migration-only until parity is established.
7. **Notion:** retain existing MacroPulse, Gate and company-tracker records as research stores. Editorial Skill/Notion page is a discoverable guidance mirror, not a separate research database or replacement for GitHub canonical docs.

**No new scheduler, source admission path, live publication or backend schema change is authorised by this standard.** Documentation does not prove generator or automation integration.

## 9. Required outputs and QA

For a **new** researched feature: original thesis, factual evidence ledger, 1–2 evidence-bearing financial/technical comparisons, 2–3 natural learning moments where valuable, a specific countercase, and an actionable conditional ending. HTML can use responsive charts/cards, but design must not distract from a readable article.

For an **edit** to an existing LY article: preserve paragraphs/charts/links first, deliver a minimal revision; provide a clearly separate “Shark Tank narrative alternative” if requested. Do not quietly replace the published version.

Before delivery, verify:

- [ ] The opening contains a genuine decision and a verified figure/claim.
- [ ] Each key figure identifies period, units, basis, previous comparable value, date and provenance.
- [ ] Calculated ratios reconcile to the source; one-time gains and non-GAAP bases are disclosed.
- [ ] Each company has a concrete bull case and the strongest credible countercase.
- [ ] Technical levels retain instrument, timeframe and observation vintage.
- [ ] Learning moments explain actual mechanisms and include relevant failure conditions.
- [ ] The author's genuine view is preserved; no manufactured “I” claims.
- [ ] Previously supplied links and chart images are unchanged on a light edit.
- [ ] The conclusion says what confirms or invalidates the interpretation and what comes next.
- [ ] HTML/DOCX are rendered and checked on desktop/mobile as applicable.
- [ ] Publication and automatic plugin/schedule consumption are NOT claimed without readback/test receipts.

## 10. Copyable prompt for the writing engine

> Apply Bluebelly's fundamentals-led Investor Test alongside the canonical Story-Driven Daily Report Standard and the LY Style Guide. Start with the verified market dilemma, then follow the pitch → economic evidence → strongest objection → changed interpretation → conditional verdict sequence. For each company, select only fundamentals that change a decision: comparable growth, operating economics, cash conversion, investment spend, expectations and valuation where verified. Explain each surprising contrast exactly when the reader encounters it, using plain British English, short paragraphs and natural first-person LY commentary only when actually supplied. Keep the original technical setup, figures, charts and existing hyperlinks for author-provided edits; do not add links or substantially rewrite their flow without a separate request. Distinguish issuer-reported numbers, consensus estimates, accounting one-offs, assumptions and future scenarios. End with observable support/resistance, financial catalysts and explicit invalidation. Keep citations and vintage in the evidence ledger, and preserve reader-facing clarity. Do not invent statistics, valuations, quotes or author opinions.
