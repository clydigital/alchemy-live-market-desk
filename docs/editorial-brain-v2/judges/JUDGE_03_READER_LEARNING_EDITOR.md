# Judge 03: Reader and Learning Editor

## Persona

A product editor focused on how quickly an analyst can absorb, retain and reuse market information.

The judge values:

- clarity;
- narrative flow;
- cognitive load;
- usefulness for writing;
- meaningful interaction;
- retention;
- mobile readability;
- the ability to move from summary to depth without losing context.

The judge dislikes decorative gamification, dense unprioritised dashboards and long generic summaries.

## Scoring method

Each design is scored from 1 to 10 on:

- Immediate comprehension
- Narrative strength
- Depth when needed
- Cognitive load
- Memory and review
- Writing usefulness
- Mobile experience
- Engagement without gimmicks

## Live winner

# Variant A: Editorial Triage Grid

## Why it wins

Variant A creates the clearest path from information to action.

The hierarchy is easy to understand:

- what changed;
- what matters;
- what comes next;
- what to write;
- what can wait.

This structure reduces the feeling of being surrounded by useful modules without knowing where to begin.

It also gives the analyst enough detail to form an article plan without forcing a full deep dive.

## Why Variant B does not win

Variant B is excellent for deliberate macro work, but the large component matrices and historical comparisons create a heavier first-use experience.

A user may learn more about a release but still spend too long deciding whether it is today’s strongest story.

## Why Variant C does not win

Variant C provides the best research narrative, but the graph, evidence room and timeline can produce too many simultaneous choices.

The user may understand the story deeply but lose the fast daily workflow.

## Hybrid winner

# Variant B: Interactive Casefile

## Tie-break decision

Variant A and Variant B receive the same numerical score.

Variant B wins the tie because the Hybrid is intended to make complex research easier and more enjoyable to absorb, not merely shorter.

The Casefile format turns the most valuable parts of the research method into a natural learning sequence:

- event;
- market belief;
- accepted case;
- evidence;
- missing variable;
- mechanism;
- next test.

That is more memorable than a standard morning programme and still avoids shallow game mechanics.

## Why Variant A narrowly loses

Variant A is the easiest daily habit and the strongest mobile briefing.

However, it risks becoming another polished summary layer if users do not open the deeper evidence.

## Why Variant C does not win

Variant C has strong session flexibility and review mechanics.

Its mission framing adds cognitive overhead and may become repetitive for an experienced analyst.

The user asked for edutainment, but the entertainment should come from revealing the story, not from completing tasks.

## Final reader-experience decision

- **Live Market Desk:** Variant A, Editorial Triage Grid
- **Hybrid Market Desk:** Variant B, Interactive Casefile

These are separate selections. This judge is not combining the designs.
