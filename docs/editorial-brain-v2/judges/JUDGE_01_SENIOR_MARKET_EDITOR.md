# Judge 01: Senior Market Editor

## Persona

A senior cross-asset market editor responsible for deciding what Alchemy should publish today.

The judge values:

- timeliness;
- specificity;
- strong sourcing;
- clear expectations framing;
- market impact;
- next-catalyst usefulness;
- article readiness;
- low time-to-decision.

The judge does not reward visual novelty unless it improves editorial decisions.

## Scoring method

Each design is scored from 1 to 10 on:

- Timeliness
- Relevance ranking
- Specificity
- Research credibility
- Editorial usefulness
- Release handling
- Historical usefulness
- Reader speed

## Live winner

# Variant A: Editorial Triage Grid

## Why it wins

The central failure being corrected is not a lack of stored research. It is the desk failing to surface the timely, specific and publishable development.

Variant A solves that problem most directly.

It leads with:

- what changed;
- what matters now;
- what comes next;
- whether the story is ready for OB or COTD.

It is the most likely design to surface an Oman negotiation, a contradictory official statement, a major data release or a material market move before the analyst opens another chat.

The release module is less complete than Variant B, but it is still sufficient for daily editorial work and can link to full release pages without making releases the homepage’s permanent centre.

## Why Variant B does not win

Variant B is the strongest macro release product.

However, it risks repeating the current problem in a different form: a technically excellent system that does not always tell the editor which story deserves attention now.

It would be excellent for a dedicated Macro Data tab, but this judge must choose one complete Live design.

## Why Variant C does not win

Variant C offers the strongest narrative research and contradiction handling.

Its weakness is speed. The graph and case structure may require too much interpretation before the editor reaches the practical decision.

For long-form and geopolitical research it is excellent. For a daily command centre it is slower than Variant A.

## Hybrid winner

# Variant A: Market Morning Show

## Why it wins

The Hybrid’s role is to make the Original easier and faster to absorb.

Variant A gives the strongest daily sequence:

- Since You Were Away
- Today’s Lead
- Why the Market Reacted
- What Does Not Fit
- Mechanism in Motion
- Today, Tonight and Later
- Data Drop
- Three Things to Remember

This flow is immediately useful and still includes contradiction, mechanism and next catalyst.

It is the best fit for the user’s complaint that the desk contains many tools but fails to present a strong centrepiece.

## Why Variant B does not win

Variant B is the strongest deep-dive format.

It loses because it is not the best default entry experience. The user would still need to choose a case before receiving a full daily briefing.

## Why Variant C does not win

Variant C is engaging but introduces more steps between the analyst and the information.

The mission framing may be useful occasionally, but it is not the fastest way to prepare an Opening Bell article.

## Final editorial decision

- **Live Market Desk:** Variant A, Editorial Triage Grid
- **Hybrid Market Desk:** Variant A, Market Morning Show

These choices should remain separate designs. The judge is not recommending a merged interface.
