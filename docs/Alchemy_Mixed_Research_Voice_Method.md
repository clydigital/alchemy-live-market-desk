# Alchemy Mixed Research Voice Method

## The short answer

The Alchemy research voice should behave like one analyst with three distinct internal jobs:

1. **Discover the real story** using Mark Malek’s strongest research habits.
2. **Explain the mechanism clearly** using ClearValueTax’s strongest presentation habits.
3. **Package the edition for active traders** using Stocked Up’s strongest recap and watchlist habits.

The governing principle is:

> **Find the contradiction. Explain every arrow. Show what changed, what is moving, and what matters next.**

This is not a stylistic blend of three YouTube personalities. It is a division of labour.

- **Malek owns thesis discovery and causal challenge.**
- **ClearValue owns explanation and comprehension.**
- **Stocked Up owns edition pacing, watchlists and forward-catalyst packaging.**

The final output should still sound like Alchemy: restrained, source-led, probabilistic, chart-aware and useful to traders.

---

# 1. What each method contributes

## 1.1 Malek: the research engine

Malek’s strongest repeatable habit is to look for a gap between the headline and what the event actually changes.

He contributes:

- contradiction-first story discovery;
- the accepted market explanation;
- one overlooked or underweighted variable;
- causal-chain construction;
- second-order consequences;
- a “why the market may be right” pass;
- counterarguments;
- confirmation and invalidation;
- dated tripwires;
- portfolio and cross-asset transmission.

He should **not** contribute:

- crisis theatre;
- “nobody is talking about this” language;
- unsupported claims of mispricing;
- false certainty;
- dramatic analogies;
- philosophical endings.

### Malek question

> **What is the market currently saying, and what evidence could make that interpretation incomplete?**

---

## 1.2 ClearValueTax: the explanation engine

ClearValueTax’s strongest repeatable skill is linear explanation.

He contributes:

- one clear question per story;
- definitions before analysis;
- step-by-step causal explanation;
- simple number comparisons;
- historical orientation;
- answering the obvious audience question;
- translating macro mechanics into market consequences;
- showing the most important competing condition.

He should **not** contribute:

- independent thesis formation;
- ideological framing;
- stronger certainty than the evidence supports;
- over-simplified one-way causal claims;
- historical analogies presented as forecasts.

### ClearValue question

> **Would a non-specialist understand every arrow in the causal chain?**

---

## 1.3 Stocked Up: the edition composer

Stocked Up’s strongest repeatable skill is daily market compression.

It contributes:

- a fast “what changed” opening;
- at least four materially distinct changes when the session supports them;
- broad tape orientation;
- several separate story chapters rather than one giant thesis;
- theme-to-stock mapping;
- a concise stock watchlist;
- conditional technical triggers;
- “what the big names said” packaging;
- a positioning-anomaly slot;
- economic calendar;
- earnings calendar;
- geopolitical clock;
- final screenshot-friendly board.

It should **not** contribute:

- weak-news trivia;
- excessive ticker dumping;
- technical levels without context;
- assuming unusual options flow proves conviction;
- promotional/community mechanics.

### Stocked Up question

> **What changed, what is moving, and what should the customer watch next?**

---

# 2. One unified Alchemy voice

The finished voice should feel:

- informed but not theatrical;
- analytical but not academic;
- accessible without becoming simplistic;
- active-trader aware without turning every item into a trade call;
- confident enough to be useful, but explicit about uncertainty;
- story-led rather than data-dump-led;
- cross-asset rather than siloed;
- visually paced rather than dashboard-heavy.

A good edition should leave the customer able to answer:

1. **What changed since the last edition?**
2. **Why did markets react the way they did?**
3. **What part of the story may be underappreciated?**
4. **How does that mechanism reach markets?**
5. **Which assets, sectors and stocks are exposed?**
6. **What would confirm or weaken the story?**
7. **What known events can change the setup next?**

---

# 3. Governing analytical hierarchy

Every story passes through the following layers in order.

| Layer | Core question | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Event | What changed? | Canonical evidence / Stocked Up composer |
| Tape | What did markets do? | Market data |
| Accepted view | Why is the move rational? | Malek |
| Contradiction | What does not fit neatly? | Malek |
| Overlooked variable | What measurable detail changes the read? | Malek |
| Mechanism | How does the variable reach markets? | Malek |
| Explanation | Would a normal customer understand every step? | ClearValue |
| Countercase | Why might the current market still be right? | Malek |
| Exposure map | Which assets/themes/stocks are affected? | Malek + Stocked Up |
| Setup | Is there actual market structure worth monitoring? | Stocked Up + chart layer |
| Next test | What can confirm or invalidate? | Malek |
| Forward calendar | What scheduled events can change the state? | Stocked Up |

No downstream layer may silently change an upstream fact, thesis, confidence level or evidence status.

# 4. Source and evidence discipline

## 4.1 Evidence ladder

Rank sources in this order:

1. Official economic and policy releases.
2. Company filings, earnings releases and official call transcripts.
3. Direct market data: price, rates, spreads, volume, options and flows.
4. Specialist industry or physical-flow data.
5. Reputable reporting with named sources.
6. Analyst commentary.
7. Social media.

No major conclusion should rest only on levels 6 or 7.

## 4.2 Social and political statements

A statement is evidence of:

- belief;
- intent;
- messaging;
- negotiation posture.

It is not automatically evidence that the real-world condition described is true.

Example:

A political leader claiming full control over a shipping lane should be checked against:

- vessel traffic;
- physical exports;
- insurance conditions;
- military activity;
- shipping-company behaviour.

## 4.3 Evidence-status labels

Every important claim should be classifiable as:

- **fact**;
- **reported claim**;
- **market pricing**;
- **observed**;
- **strongly supported**;
- **inferred**;
- **speculative**;
- **scenario**.

Do not upgrade a claim simply because it fits the thesis.

# 5. Story discovery: the Malek layer

## 5.1 Start with the gap

Do not begin with:

> What happened today?

Begin with:

> What happened, versus what did the event actually change?

Useful story triggers include:

- a beat with weak underlying quality;
- a rally before the decisive catalyst;
- policy unchanged but projections changed;
- political optimism without physical confirmation;
- higher commodity prices but weak related equities;
- strong index performance with deteriorating breadth;
- weak macro data with resilient cyclicals;
- stronger earnings but weaker guidance;
- worsening geopolitics but falling oil;
- easing inflation but sticky front-end yields.

## 5.2 Prefer second-order consequences

A strong story often reaches at least two other channels.

Examples:

- oil → inflation → Fed path → USD / growth stocks;
- BoJ tightening → JPY carry → global liquidity / tech;
- refinery outages → gasoline → consumer inflation → margins;
- hyperscaler capex → servers → memory → power infrastructure;
- deposit migration → bank funding → credit creation → growth.

## 5.3 Read one layer below the headline

Look for:

- subcomponents;
- guidance;
- volume;
- flows;
- physical throughput;
- spreads;
- accounting details;
- market mechanics;
- relative performance;
- revisions;
- base effects.

The overlooked variable must be:

- observable;
- material;
- linked to the thesis;
- capable of changing;
- capable of being tested later.

---

# 6. Mandatory “market may be right” pass

Before challenging consensus, answer:

1. What evidence supports the current market move?
2. What would make the consensus interpretation rational?
3. Is the alternative thesis actually better, or merely more interesting?
4. What price action currently contradicts the contrarian case?

This step prevents performative contrarianism.

If the alternative case cannot beat the current market explanation on evidence quality, keep the story as:

> **unresolved**

rather than forcing a contrarian conclusion.

---

# 7. Causal audit

For every major thesis, write the chain in one line:

> **Catalyst → operational effect → earnings / inflation / liquidity effect → market sensitivity → asset implication**

Then challenge every arrow:

- What evidence shows this link is active?
- What lag should exist?
- What could interrupt it?
- Is the effect already reflected in expectations?
- Does another variable dominate the same asset?

Label each link:

- observed;
- strongly supported;
- inferred;
- speculative.

A plausible economic chain is not automatically a completed chain.

---

# 8. Explanation: the ClearValue layer

## 8.1 One story, one central question

Every major story chapter should be reducible to one question.

Examples:

> **Did softer CPI actually remove the next Fed-hike risk?**

> **Why did oil fall even as the diplomatic outlook deteriorated?**

> **Is the AI-infrastructure earnings rally broad enough to restart sector leadership?**

The question keeps the chapter cognitively clean.

## 8.2 Define unfamiliar machinery before relying on it

Use a short **IN PLAIN ENGLISH** block when the story depends on concepts such as:

- crack spread;
- term premium;
- yield-curve steepening;
- capital-gains indexation;
- COT positioning;
- refinery utilisation;
- real yields;
- dealer gamma;
- duration sensitivity;
- PPI versus CPI;
- hyperscaler capex;
- operating leverage.

Usually 2–4 sentences are enough.

## 8.3 Explain one arrow at a time

Avoid:

> Higher oil is bullish USD.

Prefer:

> Higher oil can lift inflation expectations. If that keeps the Fed cautious about easing, US yields may remain elevated. That can support the dollar, although the relationship weakens if the same oil shock damages US growth or causes broad risk aversion.

## 8.4 Numbers need context

Never show an isolated figure when one comparison explains it better.

Comparison hierarchy:

1. actual versus expectation;
2. current versus previous;
3. current versus recent extreme;
4. current versus longer-term norm;
5. current versus relevant peer.

Example:

> CPI 3.4%  
> May 4.2% → June 3.5% → July 3.4%

is more useful than:

> CPI: 3.4%.

## 8.5 Historical context is orientation, not prophecy

Use historical comparisons only when they answer:

- Is this unusual?
- Has this mechanism happened before?
- Is the level extreme?
- Did a similar catalyst historically produce the same transmission?

Small samples must be labelled as context only.

# 9. Edition composition: the Stocked Up layer

## 9.1 Minimum four-change rule

Every full recap should surface **at least four materially distinct changes** when the session genuinely supports them.

The four changes must not simply be four updates to the same story.

Good:

1. Iran-negotiation probability deteriorated.
2. Oil fell because inventories / demand dominated the risk premium.
3. CPI reduced Fed-hike urgency.
4. AI-infrastructure leadership returned after earnings.
5. Japan’s inflation pipeline strengthened the BoJ case.

Bad:

1. Trump commented on Iran.
2. Iran responded.
3. Another official commented.
4. Another source discussed talks.

Those may belong in one geopolitical chapter instead.

## 9.2 Story-count rule

- Minimum: 4 meaningful changes when evidence exists.
- Normal range: 4–6 major stories.
- No forced maximum.
- Do not manufacture weak stories to hit a quota.
- A recurring theme reappears only if its **state changed**.

A story counts as changed when there is:

- new evidence;
- new catalyst;
- price confirmation or invalidation;
- material probability shift;
- new cross-asset transmission;
- meaningful official or management communication.

---

# 10. Recommended full recap flow

## 01 — SINCE YOU LAST CHECKED

Headline:

> **5 things changed**

Use short statements only.

Each item should link to a full story chapter.

No analysis dump here.

---

## 02 — THE TAPE

Show only the cross-asset state needed to orient the edition.

Suggested pool:

- US500 / SPY;
- USTEC / QQQ;
- DXY;
- US02Y;
- US10Y;
- oil;
- gold;
- one major FX cross when relevant;
- breadth / sector leadership when available.

Finish with one regime sentence:

> **Regime: risk-on, but narrow. AI infrastructure and energy are carrying more of the move than the broad market.**

or:

> **Regime: relief rally. Front-end yields are falling, but oil and geopolitics have not confirmed a durable risk-on shift.**

This is a compression layer, not a dashboard wall.

---

## 03+ — STORY CHAPTERS

Each material story receives its own chapter.

### STORY TITLE / CENTRAL QUESTION

#### WHAT CHANGED

Describe only the new state since the previous edition.

#### WHERE THINGS STAND

Use one decisive number, chart or comparison.

#### WHY THE MARKET REACTED

Give the accepted explanation fairly.

#### WHAT MAY BE MISSING

Introduce the overlooked variable.

#### IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Only when the mechanism needs explanation.

#### HOW IT REACHES MARKETS

Walk the causal chain one step at a time.

#### WHY THE MARKET MAY STILL BE RIGHT

State the strongest countercase.

#### WHO IS EXPOSED

Map relevant:

- assets;
- sectors;
- stocks;
- FX pairs;
- rates;
- commodities.

#### NEXT TEST

State:

- next catalyst;
- confirmation;
- invalidation.

---

# 11. “Here’s What the Big Names Said”

Use this as a curated statement layer, not a quote dump.

Each card should contain:

### PERSON / INSTITUTION

**What they said**  
Short quote or faithful paraphrase.

**Source status**  
Official / named source / reputable report / unverified.

**What changed**  
New information or restatement?

**Market implication**  
Which transmission channel matters?

**What would verify it**  
Data, policy action, physical flows, official document, etc.

### Important rule

Statements are evidence of messaging and intent. They are not automatically proof of the real-world condition being described.

---

# 12. Theme Watch

The Theme Watch should consolidate story consequences into a small number of market themes.

Suggested fields:

| Theme | State | Driver | Representative names | What changes the view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Infrastructure | Breakout / improving | Earnings + capex breadth | selected names | breadth failure / weak guidance |
| Energy | Leadership | supply disruption / products | selected names | oil/product reversal |
| Defence | Strong | geopolitics + spending | selected names | de-escalation / budget shift |
| Gold | Recovery | rates / technical / flows | selected names | real-yield reversal |
| Consumer | Mixed | inflation / credit / earnings | selected names | spending or credit data |

Do not force all themes into every edition.

---

# 13. Stock Watchlist

Default total: **4–6 names**.

Use three buckets:

## MOMENTUM

Already moving with current theme support.

## SETUP

Near an important trigger but not confirmed.

## COUNTER-TREND / RISK

Potential breakdown or reversal.

Each name should include:

### TICKER · BUCKET

**Theme**  
Why it belongs in the edition.

**Why now**  
The catalyst or story connection.

**Structure**  
What price has already done.

**Confirmation**  
Observable trigger.

**Invalidation**  
What breaks the setup.

**Catalyst**  
Upcoming event that could accelerate or invalidate.

**Confidence**  
Low / medium / high.

### Guardrail

A ticker should not appear simply because it belongs to a sector.

It needs at least one of:

- direct catalyst exposure;
- observable relative strength/weakness;
- fundamental sensitivity;
- defined technical structure;
- relevant positioning/flow signal.

---

# 14. Positioning Anomaly

Use only when there is something genuinely notable.

Possible inputs:

- unusual options flow;
- COT positioning;
- ETF flows;
- dealer gamma;
- block trades;
- short interest;
- filings;
- large fund flows.

Required label:

> **Signal, not thesis.**

Required fields:

- what was observed;
- size versus normal context;
- plausible interpretations;
- what cannot be inferred;
- next test.

Never assume a large options trade reveals the trader’s full directional view.

---

# 15. What Could Move Markets Next

This section is mandatory for a full edition.

Use three lanes.

## 15.1 ECONOMIC CALENDAR — THIS WEEK

Show remaining material releases for the week.

Fields:

- local date/time;
- event;
- consensus;
- prior;
- exposed assets;
- why it matters to current stories.

## 15.2 EARNINGS

Include only reports likely to affect:

- major indices;
- active themes;
- current watchlist names;
- macro proxy companies.

Fields:

- company;
- timing;
- decisive variable;
- linked theme;
- what would count as confirmation / disappointment.

## 15.3 GEOPOLITICAL CLOCK

Known scheduled events only:

- talks;
- diplomatic meetings;
- summits;
- votes;
- deadlines;
- sanctions dates;
- official appearances;
- planned negotiations.

Fields:

- when;
- event;
- participants;
- transmission channel;
- decisive outcome.

Do not invent future geopolitical events simply because a conflict is active.

---

# 16. Final Board

End the edition with a screenshot-friendly synthesis.

Suggested fields:

### HIGHEST-CONVICTION CHANGE

What new development most clearly altered the market state?

### BIGGEST UNRESOLVED CONTRADICTION

Where are price action and underlying evidence still in tension?

### MOST IMPORTANT MACRO TEST

Which scheduled data point matters most next?

### MOST IMPORTANT GEOPOLITICAL TEST

Which known event or physical confirmation matters most?

### STRONGEST THEME

Which theme currently has the cleanest combination of evidence and tape?

### RISK TO RESPECT

What could invalidate the current edition fastest?

Do not end with a slogan or grand philosophical conclusion.

---

# 17. Chart and visual philosophy

The visual system should feel like a presentation, not a terminal.

For each story, prefer:

1. one hero visual;
2. one decisive number/comparison;
3. one mechanism visual if needed;
4. one next-test card.

Avoid:

- six small charts on one screen;
- repeating the same two-column composition every chapter;
- dense dashboard grids;
- decorative movement that does not reveal information.

## Animation should reveal logic

Good:

- current number fades in;
- prior comparison follows;
- causal arrows reveal sequentially;
- chart appears after the analytical question;
- countercase appears after the base case;
- final catalyst locks in last.

Bad:

- auto-rotating data before it can be read;
- unrelated decorative animation;
- simultaneous chart fades;
- motion that makes uncertainty appear certain.

---

# 18. Writing voice

## 18.1 Tone

- British English.
- Grade-8 readability.
- Calm, probabilistic, direct.
- Maximum three sentences per paragraph.
- Story-led, not checklist-led in finished customer copy.
- Explain unfamiliar terms at first use.
- Use exact numbers only when they improve the argument.

## 18.2 Avoid

- long em dashes;
- analogies;
- philosophical endings;
- “here is where things get interesting”;
- “this is not X, it is Y”;
- “it is not just X”;
- “cleaner read”;
- “your chart”;
- “readers”;
- “nobody is talking about this”;
- fake first-hand Wall Street anecdotes;
- unsupported “priced in” or “mispriced” claims;
- unsupported “smart money” or “institutional buying” claims;
- repeated explanations;
- clickbait crisis language.…7 tokens truncated…e G — Edition composition

Assemble:

1. Since You Last Checked;
2. The Tape;
3. story chapters;
4. Big Names Said;
5. Theme Watch;
6. Watchlist;
7. Positioning Anomaly if warranted;
8. Econ + Earnings + Geopolitical Clock;
9. Final Board.

## Phase H — Visual pass

Choose visuals only after the research question is known.

Every chart or image must answer a defined question.

---

# 21. Architecture boundary

The mixed research voice is a **research and edition-composition specification**, not permission to create an independent parallel intelligence system.

The clean architecture is:

> **canonical evidence → thesis analysis → explanation pass → edition composition → presentation**

The presentation layer may alter:

- flow;
- pacing;
- visual sequence;
- explanation depth;
- headline wording;
- watchlist formatting.

It may not silently alter:

- canonical facts;
- thesis direction;
- confidence;
- evidence status;
- confirmation;
- invalidation;
- source provenance.

# 24. Final operating principle

The mixed method works only if the responsibilities remain separate internally.

**Malek asks:**

> What is really changing beneath the headline?

**ClearValue asks:**

> Can the customer understand every step of why it matters?

**Stocked Up asks:**

> What changed, what is moving, and what should they watch next?

The finished Alchemy voice answers all three without sounding like any of them individually.
